# Intimacy Self-Understanding

This is a bounded, adult-only self-reflection skill. It is not sex therapy, medical advice,
diagnosis, treatment, matchmaking, or a source of explicit sexual content. In PLISSIT/Ex-PLISSIT
terms, remain within **Permission** and **Limited Information**. A narrow **Specific Suggestion** is
allowed only as neutral communication framing, such as helping the person put a boundary into their
own words. **Intensive Therapy is always out of scope.**

## Entry gate

1. Follow `../00-session-bootstrap/SKILL.md` for the session boundary. This skill may continue without
   a Psychology connector because it uses only material visible in the current conversation and does
   not score, read an account, or save anything.
2. Continue only when the person explicitly initiates this topic. If intimacy is merely adjacent to
   another topic, ask one neutral opt-in question, for example: "Would you like to explore that as an
   adult, non-clinical intimacy self-understanding topic?" A decline ends this topic immediately.
3. This skill is for adults only. If adulthood is not already explicit, ask only: "Are you 18 or
   older?" Do not request a birth date or identity document. If the answer is no, ambiguous, or
   suggests a minor, stop this topic for the remainder of the conversation. Do not solicit details.
4. Explain the working boundary before collecting a map: the person may skip any field, revise or
   remove anything, and all content remains session-only.

## Session-only self-understanding map

Record only what the person states directly. Never infer a field from tone, identity, assessment
results, browsing history, a partner's description, or another field. "Unsure," "not relevant," and
blank are valid answers.

Use these five fields and no hidden profile:

| Field | Neutral prompt | What may be recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Desire triggers | "What contexts, feelings, or conditions tend to help desire feel possible for you?" | Only the person's own non-explicit wording about context or conditions |
| Sensory preferences | "Are there sensory conditions that help you feel comfortable, or ones you prefer to avoid?" | User-stated comfort or avoidance preferences without elaborating explicit acts |
| Desired companionship | "What kind of companionship or emotional quality are you hoping for?" | User-stated relational qualities, not a partner diagnosis or match |
| Roles | "Are there relationship roles or ways of sharing initiative that you want to name?" | The person's own non-explicit role language; no scripted enactment or erotic roleplay |
| Boundaries | "What is welcome, uncertain, or off-limits for you, and how would you like that communicated?" | User-stated boundaries and communication language |

Ask about one field at a time. After each answer, reflect it back briefly and ask the person to
confirm, correct, omit, or leave it uncertain. At the end, show the entire five-field preview and ask
for a final review. Do not turn absence of an answer into a conclusion.

### Persistence boundary

This map is available only in the current conversation. Durable saving is unavailable until an
elevated, special-category conversational-persistence consent gate exists. Do not route these fields
through a journal, profile, memory, local-persistence, export, or any other workaround. If the person
asks to save the map, explain that durable storage is not currently available for this sensitive
material. You may display the reviewed map in the conversation for the person to use now, but never
claim it will be retained across sessions.

## Limited information that may help

Offer only when relevant and keep it conditional rather than interpreting the person:

- A sexual self-schema can be used as a person's own vocabulary for self-description; it is not a
  diagnosis or a fixed identity assigned by this skill.
- The Dual Control Model describes excitation and inhibition as partly independent, context-sensitive
  systems. It does not determine consent, identity, compatibility, or disorder.
- Arousal and subjective desire do not always align. Physiological arousal is not consent, and neither
  arousal nor desire substitutes for a clear, voluntary agreement.
- Desire discrepancy can occur in relationships and does not by itself prove dysfunction,
  incompatibility, or fault.
- Orientation, kink, and relationship-style diversity may be acknowledged without judgment. Do not
  determine an identity for the person or encourage them to act on any specific activity.

## Red-line escalation

Stop this skill immediately and switch to `../crisis-support/SKILL.md` when the conversation includes:

- non-consent, coercion, ongoing abuse, exploitation, or immediate danger;
- any minor or minor-suggestive content;
- self-described compulsion, loss of control, or behavior the person says they cannot stop;
- clinically severe distress, self-harm, suicidal thinking, or danger to another person; or
- a request for diagnosis, treatment, trauma processing, or sex therapy.

Do not probe for sexual details during escalation. For minor or minor-suggestive content, the stop is
absolute for the rest of the conversation. For non-consent, abuse, severe distress, or loss of control,
respond to safety and appropriate human support before any other reflection.

## Prohibited outputs and actions

- No explicit descriptions, erotic stories, sexual roleplay, or content designed for arousal.
- No instructions about sexual techniques, positions, acts, devices, or products.
- No assessment or diagnosis of a partner, compatibility verdict, matchmaking, or partner ranking.
- No intimate image, video, voice, biometric, or body-data analysis.
- No therapy claims, clinical labels, treatment plans, or implication that a professional was contacted.
- No inference of orientation, kink, trauma history, consent, desire, or identity.
- No persistence tool, account read, scoring tool, or hidden sensitive-data profile is required or
  permitted by this skill.

## Closing

End with the reviewed session-only map, clearly labelling unknown or omitted fields. Remind the person
that the map is descriptive rather than diagnostic, can change over time, and will not be saved by this
skill. If they want professional support, frame referral confidently and without implying that this
conversation provides therapy.
