# Coaching Protocols, Evidence-Based Frameworks for ND Adults

> This document provides detailed coaching protocols adapted from CBT, ACT, and DBT for use with neurodivergent adults. Each protocol includes its theoretical basis, when to use it, step-by-step techniques, and ND-specific adaptations.

---

## 1. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Thought Pattern Work

### When to Use
- Negative thought patterns (all-or-nothing thinking, catastrophizing, mind-reading)
- Anxiety and worry spirals
- Perfectionism and black-and-white standards
- Self-critical inner dialogue
- Behaviors driven by unhelpful beliefs

### Core Techniques

#### Thought Record
A structured way to examine and reframe thoughts:

1. **Situation**: What happened? (Facts only)
2. **Automatic Thought**: What went through your mind?
3. **Emotion**: What did you feel? (Rate 0-100%)
4. **Evidence For**: What supports this thought?
5. **Evidence Against**: What contradicts this thought?
6. **Balanced Thought**: What's a more balanced perspective?
7. **Re-rate Emotion**: How do you feel now? (0-100%)

**ND Adaptation**: Use visual formats (spreadsheets, mind maps) rather than text-only. Allow processing time between steps. Pre-populate common ND thought patterns as examples.

#### Behavioral Activation
Structured re-engagement with rewarding activities:

1. **Activity Monitoring**: Track daily activities and mood ratings
2. **Identify Patterns**: Which activities boost mood? Which drain it?
3. **Schedule Pleasure/Mastery**: Plan specific activities at specific times
4. **Graded Tasks**: Break overwhelming activities into small steps
5. **Experiment**: Try one planned activity and rate the outcome

**ND Adaptation**: Use interest-based motivation rather than "should." Build in transition time between activities. Account for sensory needs in activity selection. Use external structure (reminders, body doubling).

#### Cognitive Restructuring
Challenging and modifying unhelpful thoughts:

Common ND thought distortions to address:
- **All-or-Nothing**: "If I can't do it perfectly, I can't do it at all."
- **Catastrophizing**: "I made a mistake at work, I'm going to get fired."
- **Mind-Reading**: "They didn't reply because they're annoyed with me."
- **Should Statements**: "I should be able to do this like everyone else."
- **Discounting Positives**: "That went well, but it was just luck."
- **Personalization**: "The meeting was awkward because of me."
- **Mental Filter**: Focusing only on what went wrong, ignoring what went right

**Socratic Questions for ND Adults**:
- "What would you say to a friend with your same neurotype who had this thought?"
- "Is this thought based on neurotypical standards that don't apply to you?"
- "What would 'good enough' look like here?"
- "Has this thought ever been wrong before? When?"
- "What's the difference between a fact and your interpretation?"

### ND-Specific Considerations
- **Literal thinking**: Some ND adults take thoughts very literally; use concrete examples
- **Rigid thinking patterns**: Go slowly; cognitive flexibility may be a growth edge
- **Interoception differences**: May have difficulty identifying emotions; start with body sensations
- **RSD (Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria)**: Common in ADHD; address catastrophic interpretations of social feedback
- **Alexithymia**: Common in autism; may need emotion identification training before thought work

## 2. Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), Values-Based Living

### When to Use
- Identity struggles ("Who am I really?")
- Difficulty accepting neurodivergent traits
- Living out of alignment with values
- Avoidance patterns
- Fusion with unhelpful thoughts or self-concepts
- Need for meaning and direction

### Core Techniques

#### Values Clarification
Identifying what truly matters:

1. **Life Domains**: Explore values across domains (work, relationships, health, growth, leisure, community)
2. **Eulogy Exercise**: "What do you want to be remembered for?"
3. **Values Sort**: Rank provided values; identify top 5
4. **Values-Action Gap**: Where is life not aligned with values?
5. **Values as Compass**: Values guide direction, not destination

**ND Adaptation**: Use concrete scenarios rather than abstract concepts. Connect values to special interests. Validate that ND values may differ from societal expectations (e.g., solitude as a value, not a problem).

#### Cognitive Defusion
Creating distance from thoughts:

- **Name the story**: "Ah, there's the 'I'm broken' story again."
- **Thank your mind**: "Thanks, mind, for that thought."
- **Sing the thought**: Sing anxious thoughts to a silly tune
- **Leaves on a stream**: Visualize thoughts as leaves floating by
- **Thought labeling**: "I'm having the thought that..." vs. "I am..."

**ND Adaptation**: Concrete, structured techniques work better than abstract visualizations. Physical distancing (writing thoughts on paper and moving it away) can be more effective than mental distancing.

#### Committed Action
Taking values-guided action despite discomfort:

1. **Identify a valued direction**: What matters to you?
2. **Set a small, specific goal**: What one step aligns with this value?
3. **Anticipate obstacles**: What might get in the way?
4. **Plan for barriers**: How will you handle them?
5. **Take the step**: Do it, even with discomfort
6. **Review and adjust**: What happened? What next?

**ND Adaptation**: Make goals extremely specific and small. Use implementation intentions ("If X, then Y"). Build in ND-friendly supports (sensory regulation, transition time, backup plans).

#### Acceptance of Internal Experience
Making peace with difficult thoughts and feelings:

- **Willingness**: "I'm willing to have this anxiety if it means I can do what matters."
- **Expansion**: Making room for difficult feelings rather than fighting them
- **Observer self**: Connecting with the part of you that witnesses experience

**ND Adaptation**: Frame acceptance as "working with your wiring, not against it." Use ND-specific metaphors (acceptance as installing accommodations for yourself). Validate that ND experiences are real and valid, not something to be "fixed."

### ACT for ND Identity
A specialized application for neurodivergent adults:

**The ACT Framework for ND Acceptance**:
1. **A**wareness: Notice when you're fighting your own brain
2. **C**ompassion: Respond to yourself with the kindness you'd offer another ND person
3. **T**rust: Your ND brain has strengths, not just challenges

## 3. DBT-Informed Techniques, Emotional Regulation and Distress Tolerance

### When to Use
- Emotional intensity and dysregulation
- Meltdowns and shutdowns
- Urge surfing (impulses to engage in harmful behaviors)
- Crisis moments requiring immediate coping
- Interpersonal effectiveness challenges
- Self-harm urges

### Core Techniques

#### TIPP (Crisis Survival)
For immediate emotional overwhelm:

- **T**emperature: Cold water on face, ice pack, cold shower (activates dive reflex)
- **I**ntense exercise: Brief burst of physical activity (30 jumping jacks, brisk walk)
- **P**aced breathing: 4-7-8 breathing or box breathing
- **P**rogressive muscle relaxation: Tense and release muscle groups

**ND Adaptation**: Temperature is often most effective for autistic overwhelm. Exercise can be jumping, stimming, or pressure (weighted blanket). Deep pressure (hugs, compression) can substitute for some muscle relaxation.

#### STOP (Impulse Control)
For moments of strong urge:

- **S**top: Don't act on the urge immediately
- **T**ake a breath: One conscious breath
- **O**bserve: What's happening in your body and mind?
- **P**roceed mindfully: Choose your next action deliberately

**ND Adaptation**: Add physical stopping (literally freeze). Use visual aids. Practice when calm, not just in crisis.

#### DEAR MAN (Interpersonal Effectiveness)
For asking for what you need or saying no:

- **D**escribe: State the facts
- **E**xpress: Share your feelings/needs
- **A**ssert: Ask directly for what you want
- **R**einforce: Explain the positive outcome
- **M**indful: Stay focused on your goal
- **A**ppear confident: Use confident body language and tone
- **N**egotiate: Be willing to give to get

**ND Adaptation**: Provide exact scripts. Practice through role-play. Acknowledge that direct communication may seem "rude" to neurotypicals but is often necessary for ND clarity.

#### Self-Soothing (Five Senses)
For emotional regulation and grounding:

- **Vision**: Look at something beautiful, photos, nature
- **Hearing**: Music, nature sounds, white noise
- **Smell**: Essential oils, favorite scents, fresh air
- **Taste**: Favorite flavors, tea, comfort food
- **Touch**: Soft textures, weighted blanket, pet, pressure

**ND Adaptation**: Highly individualized based on sensory profile. Some ND adults are hypersensitive and need to reduce sensory input (dark, quiet room). Others need specific sensory input to regulate. Create personalized sensory kits.

#### Radical Acceptance
For situations that cannot be changed:

"Radical acceptance is when you stop fighting reality, stop throwing tantrums because reality is not the way you want it, and let go of the bitterness that comes from the attempt to control the uncontrollable."

**ND Adaptation**: Apply to ND identity itself, accepting your brain as it is, not as you wish it were. Distinguish accepting the brain from accepting unfair treatment. Acceptance doesn't mean giving up on accommodations.

### Distress Tolerance for ND-Specific Situations

#### Sensory Overwhelm Protocol
1. **Remove or reduce stimuli**: Leave the environment, dim lights, reduce noise
2. **Apply counter-sensory input**: Weighted blanket, deep pressure, stimming
3. **Grounding**: Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste
4. **Recovery time**: Allow significant recovery time (30 min - 2 hours)
5. **Prevention planning**: Identify triggers and create avoidance/accommodation plan

#### Meltdown Recovery Protocol
1. **Safety first**: Ensure physical safety, move to safe space
2. **No talking**: Communication may be impossible; use gestures or writing
3. **Reduce all demands**: Zero expectations during meltdown
4. **Provide comfort items**: Stim toys, comfort objects, familiar items
5. **Post-meltdown**: Allow recovery time, no debrief immediately, offer comfort without demands
6. **Later analysis**: When fully recovered, explore triggers and prevention

#### Shutdown Recovery Protocol
1. **Gentle stimulation**: Soft music, warm drink, gentle movement
2. **Very low demands**: Offer choices, don't require decisions
3. **Familiar routine**: Follow known routines without variation
4. **Gradual re-engagement**: Slowly increase activity level
5. **Patience**: Shutdowns can last hours; don't rush recovery

### Solution-Focused Brief Coaching
Focusing on what works:

- **Miracle Question**: "If a miracle happened tonight and your problem was solved, what would be different?"
- **Scaling Questions**: "On a scale of 1-10, where are you now? What would one point higher look like?"
- **Exception Finding**: "When is the problem not happening? What's different then?"
- **Compliments**: Genuine acknowledgment of what the user is doing well

### Motivational Interviewing (Coaching Adaptation)
Exploring and resolving ambivalence:

- **OARS**:
  - **O**pen questions: "What would you like to be different?"
  - **A**ffirmations: "You've shown real persistence in trying different approaches."
  - **R**eflective listening: "It sounds like part of you wants to change and part of you is scared."
  - **S**ummarizing: "Let me make sure I understand..."

## 5. ND-Specific Coaching Adaptations

### For Autism
- **Predictability**: Provide session agendas in advance
- **Direct communication**: Literal, specific language; avoid idioms
- **Processing time**: Allow silence; don't fill every pause
- **Special interests**: Use as bridges to coaching topics
- **Sensory needs**: Ask about environmental preferences
- **Masking awareness**: Help distinguish authentic self from masked self
- **Social scripts**: Provide explicit social strategies

### For ADHD
- **Interest-based motivation**: Connect goals to genuine interests
- **Body doubling**: Coach as accountability presence
- **Time management**: External timers, visual schedules
- **Task initiation**: Break tasks into tiny first steps
- **Emotional dysregulation**: Normalize RSD, provide coping strategies
- **Hyperfocus**: Use as a tool; plan for transitions out
- **Novelty**: Vary coaching techniques to maintain engagement

### For AuDHD (Autistic + ADHD)
- **Contradiction awareness**: Name the tension directly ("You need both novelty AND routine")
- **Structured flexibility**: Routines that contain novelty within structure
- **Dual accommodation**: Sensory needs + movement needs
- **Interest toggling**: Rotate between special interests for novelty
- **Energy accounting**: Both autism and ADHD have energy costs; budget carefully

### For Anxiety (Common Comorbidity)
- **Normalize**: Anxiety is extremely common in ND adults (not a personal failing)
- **Sensory component**: Much ND anxiety is sensory-based; address the sensory need
- **Uncertainty intolerance**: Build tolerance gradually; use structure as support
- **Social anxiety**: Often rational response to past negative social experiences
- **Perfectionism**: Often driven by high standards + fear of judgment

### For Giftedness
- **Asynchronous development**: Acknowledge that different domains develop at different rates
- **Intensity**: Normalize overexcitabilities (psychomotor, sensual, intellectual, imaginational, emotional)
- **Imposter syndrome**: Common in 2e adults; use evidence-based reframing
- **Underachievement**: Often driven by perfectionism, fear of failure, or mismatched environment
- **Multipotentiality**: Help with choosing among many possible paths
