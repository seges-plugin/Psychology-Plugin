# OCEAN Facets, Technical Reference

> Technical descriptions of all 30 OCEAN facets with high/low pole characteristics and measurement
> notes. Use alongside `strength-frames.md` for measurement-bounded interpretation.

## Identity and context boundary

This is a facet reference, not a map from OCEAN responses to neurotype, giftedness, twice-exceptionality,
ADHD, autism, PDA, disability, neurological difference, clinical condition, leadership ability, or future
outcome. A pattern may be useful to discuss only as the person's responses to this measure. If a person has
explicitly named an identity or lived context and asks to include it, ask how *they* experience the pattern
in a specific setting; do not attribute, confirm, or explain the score through that identity.

---

## Domain: Openness to Experience (O)

Openness reflects cognitive flexibility, aesthetic sensitivity, intellectual curiosity, and preference for
novelty. It describes responses to these themes in this measure, not capability, identity, or diagnosis.

### O1: Imagination
- **High pole**: Rich fantasy life, enjoys mental exploration, creates vivid internal scenarios, engages in "what-if" thinking
- **Low pole**: Grounded in concrete reality, prefers facts to speculation, action-oriented, present-focused
- **Measurement**: Items tap frequency and vividness of daydreaming, fantasy engagement, creative visualization
- **Context prompt**: If the person has named a relevant context, ask whether imagination feels different across settings; do not attribute the score to that context.

### O2: Artistic Interests
- **High pole**: Deep appreciation for art, music, beauty, design; seeks aesthetic experiences; may create art
- **Low pole**: Interest in function over form; indifferent to aesthetics; practical orientation
- **Measurement**: Interest in artistic activities, aesthetic appreciation, creative pursuits
- **Context prompt**: If the person has named a relevant context, ask how surroundings affect aesthetic engagement; do not attribute the score to that context.

### O3: Emotionality
- **High pole**: Access to emotional experience, emotional awareness, depth of feeling, sentimentality
- **Low pole**: Emotional steadiness, less access to feelings, more cognitive processing of emotion
- **Measurement**: Emotional responsiveness, sentimentality, access to feelings
- **Context prompt**: Invite the person to say whether the item wording captured their emotional experience; do not use an identity or condition to reinterpret the score.

### O4: Adventurousness
- **High pole**: Seeks novelty, variety, new experiences; bored by routine; exploratory
- **Low pole**: Prefers familiarity, routine, the known; comfortable with repetition
- **Measurement**: Willingness to try new things, preference for variety vs. routine
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether novelty and routine preferences vary by setting; do not explain either pole through an identity or condition.

### O5: Intellect
- **High pole**: Love of ideas, intellectual engagement, abstract thinking, problem-solving enjoyment
- **Low pole**: Preference for concrete, practical thinking; action over analysis
- **Measurement**: Interest in abstract ideas, intellectual activities, complex problem-solving
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether interest in ideas feels distinct from the other Openness facets; do not use the contrast to infer identity or ability.

### O6: Liberalism
- **High pole**: Open to unconventional ideas, challenges tradition, independent thinking, tolerance for ambiguity
- **Low pole**: Respects tradition, prefers the familiar, conventional values, comfort with established norms
- **Measurement**: Political/social liberalism, tolerance for unconventional ideas, tradition respect
- **Context prompt**: Ask how the person relates to tradition or ambiguity in the setting they chose; do not use this facet to infer identity.

## Domain: Conscientiousness (C)

Conscientiousness reflects self-discipline, organization, goal-orientation, and deliberation. Differences
between its facets are response patterns to explore with the person, not evidence of compensation, identity,
or a condition.

### C1: Self-Efficacy
- **High pole**: Confidence in abilities, sense of competence, belief in capacity to handle challenges
- **Low pole**: Self-doubt, lower confidence in abilities, feeling less capable than others
- **Measurement**: Belief in ability to accomplish tasks, confidence in judgment
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether the person's confidence changes by task or setting; do not infer competence, identity, or a reason for the response.

### C2: Orderliness
- **High pole**: Need for organization, tidiness, structured environment; systems and routines
- **Low pole**: Comfortable with disorder, flexible environment, less need for structure
- **Measurement**: Preference for order, organization habits, tidiness
- **Context prompt**: Ask which routines or environments support the person's preferred level of order; do not explain either pole through an identity or condition.

### C3: Dutifulness
- **High pole**: Strong sense of obligation, reliability, follows rules and commitments
- **Low pole**: More autonomous, selective about obligations, less bound by external rules
- **Measurement**: Sense of duty, reliability, rule-following
- **Context prompt**: Ask which commitments feel chosen, useful, or burdensome in the person's current setting; do not infer a profile or diagnosis from this facet.

### C4: Achievement-Striving
- **High pole**: Ambitious, works hard, sets high standards, driven to accomplish
- **Low pole**: Content with modest goals, less driven by achievement, defines success personally
- **Measurement**: Ambition, goal-setting, work ethic, achievement motivation
- **Context prompt**: Ask what achievement means to the person and whether effort feels sustainable; do not treat this score as evidence of compensation or hidden distress.

### C5: Self-Discipline
- **High pole**: Persists at tasks, follows through, resists procrastination, strong willpower
- **Low pole**: Difficulty persisting, procrastinates, needs external structure, starts more than finishes
- **Measurement**: Follow-through, procrastination tendency, persistence despite difficulty
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether follow-through varies by task, support, or environment; do not attribute unevenness to an identity or condition.

### C6: Cautiousness
- **High pole**: Thinks before acting, deliberates, considers consequences, careful decision-making
- **Low pole**: Acts quickly, spontaneous, less deliberation, comfortable with risk
- **Measurement**: Deliberation before action, carefulness, impulse to reflect before acting
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether the person approaches decisions differently across stakes or settings; do not use this facet to infer anxiety, identity, or condition.

## Domain: Extraversion (E)

Extraversion reflects sociability, positive emotionality, activity level, and sensitivity to reward. Facet
differences can be discussed as setting-dependent preferences or experiences, not as evidence of an identity
or neurological explanation.

### E1: Friendliness
- **High pole**: Warm, welcoming, kind, creates positive feelings in others, approachable
- **Low pole**: More reserved, less effusive warmth, selective about social opening
- **Measurement**: Warmth, kindness, approachability, positive social feelings
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether warmth is expressed differently across relationships or settings; do not use the score to infer masking or identity.

### E2: Gregariousness
- **High pole**: Enjoys crowds, parties, social groups; energized by company; seeks social stimulation
- **Low pole**: Prefers solitude or small groups; energized by alone time; social interaction requires energy
- **Measurement**: Preference for social company, enjoyment of groups, sociability
- **Context prompt**: Ask which kinds of social contact feel energizing, neutral, or tiring; do not treat this facet as a neurological explanation or identity signal.

### E3: Assertiveness
- **High pole**: Takes charge, speaks up, comfortable leading, influences others, takes social space
- **Low pole**: Less comfortable taking charge, prefers following, quieter in groups, less socially dominant
- **Measurement**: Leadership comfort, speaking up, social dominance
- **Context prompt**: Ask how comfortable the person feels speaking up in the context they chose; do not infer identity from directness or assertiveness.

### E4: Activity Level
- **High pole**: Fast-paced, energetic, always doing something, high vitality
- **Low pole**: Slower pace, more deliberate, conserves energy, calmer rhythm
- **Measurement**: Pace of life, energy level, activity preference
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether pace differs across physical, social, and mental activities; do not attribute a pattern to identity or condition.

### E5: Excitement-Seeking
- **High pole**: Seeks thrills, novelty, stimulation; bored by routine; needs intensity
- **Low pole**: Content with calm, routine, predictable environments; low need for external stimulation
- **Measurement**: Thrill-seeking, stimulation preference, novelty craving
- **Context prompt**: Ask which settings feel stimulating or restorative to the person; do not infer sensory needs, identity, or condition.

### E6: Cheerfulness
- **High pole**: Experiences joy readily, optimistic, enthusiastic, positive emotions come easily
- **Low pole**: More serious baseline, less exuberant, positive emotions are less frequent but may be deeper
- **Measurement**: Positive affect frequency, optimism, enthusiasm
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether the response reflects a recent period or a longer pattern; do not infer mood, masking, identity, or condition.

## Domain: Agreeableness (A)

Agreeableness reflects cooperation, compassion, respect for social harmony, and trust. Differences between
facets may be useful to explore in a concrete relationship or setting; they do not establish empathy,
morality, identity, or a condition.

### A1: Trust
- **High pole**: Believes in others' good intentions, gives benefit of the doubt, open to people
- **Low pole**: Skeptical, cautious about trusting, reads situations carefully before opening up
- **Measurement**: Interpersonal trust, belief in others' sincerity
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether trust differs by relationship or setting; do not infer trauma, identity, or a cause from this score.

### A2: Morality
- **High pole**: Values honesty highly, authentic, cannot tolerate being fake, sincere
- **Low pole**: More pragmatic about honesty, comfortable with social niceties, diplomatic
- **Measurement**: Frankness, sincerity, discomfort with deception
- **Context prompt**: Ask how the person balances directness and social tact in the situation they chose; do not infer identity from this facet.

### A3: Altruism
- **High pole**: Generous, helpful, finds satisfaction in giving, puts others first
- **Low pole**: More self-interested, selective about helping, conserves resources for self
- **Measurement**: Helping behavior, generosity, other-centeredness
- **Context prompt**: Ask what helping looks like to the person in their own relationships; do not use the score to infer identity or intent.

### A4: Cooperation
- **High pole**: Compromises readily, avoids conflict, prioritizes harmony, accommodating
- **Low pole**: Independent-minded, stands ground, willing to disagree, less compromising
- **Measurement**: Conflict avoidance, compromise willingness, accommodation
- **Context prompt**: Ask how the person decides when to compromise or hold a boundary; do not diagnose, label, or explain the score through an identity.

### A5: Modesty
- **High pole**: Humble, downplays achievements, self-effacing, doesn't seek spotlight
- **Low pole**: Comfortable with self-promotion, acknowledges competence, takes credit
- **Measurement**: Humility, self-promotion comfort, egotism (inverted)
- **Context prompt**: Ask how comfortable the person feels naming their contribution in a particular setting; do not infer impostor feelings, giftedness, or identity.

### A6: Sympathy
- **High pole**: Deeply moved by others' suffering, emotional empathy, compassion, tenderness
- **Low pole**: More analytical response to others' problems, cognitive empathy over emotional empathy
- **Measurement**: Emotional empathy, compassion, tenderness
- **Context prompt**: Ask how the person notices and responds to others' difficulties; do not use this facet to infer empathy capacity or identity.

## Domain: Neuroticism (N)

Neuroticism reflects emotional sensitivity, threat detection, and stress reactivity. Use neutral,
non-pathologizing language and distinguish a self-report snapshot from clinical symptoms, identity, or cause.

### N1: Anxiety
- **High pole**: Worries frequently, anticipates threats, nervous, vigilant for danger
- **Low pole**: Calm, unworried, rarely anxious, secure in most situations
- **Measurement**: Worry frequency, nervousness, threat anticipation
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether the response reflects a current situation, a longer pattern, or both; do not infer anxiety disorder, identity, or a clinical cause.

### N2: Anger
- **High pole**: Easily frustrated, irritable, quick to anger, sensitive to provocation
- **Low pole**: Slow to anger, forgiving, rarely irritated, long fuse
- **Measurement**: Irritability, anger frequency, frustration tolerance
- **Context prompt**: Ask which situations tend to bring frustration and what helps; do not infer sensory experience, identity, or a condition.

### N3: Depression
- **High pole**: Prone to low mood, sadness, hopelessness, negative affect
- **Low pole**: Positive baseline mood, resilient, bounces back from setbacks
- **Measurement**: Depression symptoms, sadness frequency, pessimism
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether low mood is recent, persistent, or situation-specific, without labeling it. Follow the safety route for any expressed crisis rather than interpreting this facet clinically.

### N4: Self-Consciousness
- **High pole**: Sensitive to social evaluation, aware of being observed, concerned with others' opinions
- **Low pole**: Comfortable in public, unconcerned with social judgment, thick-skinned
- **Measurement**: Social anxiety, evaluation sensitivity, embarrassment proneness
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether concern about evaluation differs by audience or setting; do not infer a history, masking, identity, or condition.

### N5: Impulsiveness
- **High pole**: Acts on immediate urges, difficulty resisting temptations, speaks before thinking
- **Low pole**: Strong impulse control, thinks before acting, good at delaying gratification
- **Measurement**: Impulse control, urge resistance, deliberation
- **Context prompt**: Ask whether acting quickly or pausing varies by context; do not infer a condition or identity from either pole.

### N6: Vulnerability
- **High pole**: Feels overwhelmed by stress, panics under pressure, needs support in crises
- **Low pole**: Resilient under stress, handles pressure well, steady in crises
- **Measurement**: Stress vulnerability, panic tendency, crisis handling
- **Context prompt**: Ask which situations feel manageable or overwhelming and what support the person prefers; do not infer a sensory profile, identity, or condition.
